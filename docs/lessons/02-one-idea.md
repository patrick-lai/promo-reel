# Lesson 2: One video, one idea

**The principle.** A promo should leave a stranger with one sentence they could repeat to a friend. Not a feature list, one sentence. Every shot then has a single job: set that idea up or prove it. Features still belong in the video, but as evidence for the idea, not as separate items on a list. It works because a viewer on a feed remembers one thing at best. Give them five and they keep none, and they can't pass any of it on.

A quick test: can you say the idea in about ten words, and can you point to the line in the script that each shot proves? A shot that proves nothing is a candidate to cut, however good it looks.

**Example.** When Steve Jobs launched the first iPod in 2001, he did list specs: a 5 GB drive, FireWire about 30 times faster than USB, 10 hours of battery. But each spec answered the same question, how do you get a thousand songs into your pocket? The pocket reveal itself is one short line, "this amazing little device holds a thousand songs and it goes right in my pocket" ([the 27-second clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SUJNspeux8), from the [full 2001 event](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kN0SVBCJqLs)). The first [iPod commercial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mM6InCC_ee0) is a minute of music that ends on exactly those words, "1,000 songs in your pocket". There are plenty of specs, but only one idea.

Our reference study has a useful contrast. The Cursor 2.0 launch is deliberately a multi-feature video: four beats of about 12 to 16 s, each with its own title card. That works for an existing audience who already know the product, and it's closer to a changelog than a hero. Devin's launch is the single-idea version, with the whole claim ("the first AI software engineer") in the first spoken sentence.

**Our own cuts.** Each of our three cuts already contains a strong single idea, but they bury it to different degrees.
- Anime v11 is the cleanest. It opens on "ONE ASK. TONIGHT." and lands on "GOOD MORNING." Everything in between (the agents, checks, review, merged) is the night passing.
- Hero v5 has the best line of all three, "Go to bed. Wake up to merged PRs." But it only arrives near the end, after seven voiceover lines that walk through six different features: the plan, the crew, worktrees, checks, asking only when needed, the reviewer. Then the closer, "Every token builds something.", opens a second idea just before the end card.
- Talk show v7 states its idea early, "neither of us is going to write the code", then spends a stretch on the Workshop's tiny town, which is charming but a different idea.

**For the next commission-ai video.** Write the one sentence before the shot list, and check every shot against it in the stills round, before any render. One option (not a decision) is "Go to bed. Wake up to merged PRs." as the spine of the next hero. The feature lines would then be rewritten as reasons you can sleep: it only wakes you when it needs you, and a reviewer explains every change. "Every token builds something" and the Workshop could each be their own short feature clip later, rather than a second idea squeezed into this one.

**Your exercise.** Write commission-ai's one idea in 10 words or fewer. Then pick one of hero v5's voiceover lines that doesn't serve that sentence, and decide whether to cut it or reword it.

# Reference study: two new targets (5 Oct 2026)

Produced with `promo watch` (frames, cuts, contact sheets, audio metrics). Sources: OpenAI "Introducing dots" (uXspbC2srEQ, 148 s,
1080p24) and Anthropic "Introducing Claude Opus 5.5" (1f13Bl1sYkw, 20 s, 1080p25). Raw sheets: `promo watch <url>` rebuilds them.
Cut data for dots: `dots-openai-WATCH.md`.

## A. "dots" (OpenAI): cinematic story, warm, human

What it is: a short film, not a feature tour. Night sound stage, a woman walks in, the "workshop" lights up, faces react, and
the product (chat UI on big physical screens, with the hero mascots) is shown through *people living a day*. It ends on a
pull-back that reveals the whole set, then the logo.

Measured (watch.json): 65 cuts, mean shot 2.24 s, median 1.75 s, min 0.83, max 9.39 (the opening). Cut rate by fifth of runtime:
0.44, 0.54, 0.58, 0.34, 0.30 cuts/s: rises through the middle, then relaxes for the payoff. Audio about -19.6 LUFS (quiet, dynamic),
music is a soft pulse around 125 BPM, VO-free in the opening.

What makes it look expensive (to copy as *grade and rhythm*, since we shoot only real UI):
1. **Open from darkness.** 0-2 s near-black, a soft light, the title blooms in (rainbow-edged glow), then the room lights up. Night -> day arc.
2. **Warm, low-key grade.** Wood and amber highlights, crushed blacks, gentle bloom on lights, shallow depth of field (everything not the subject is soft).
3. **One idea per shot, slow camera.** Slow dolly / rack focus. Nothing cuts faster than 0.8 s.
4. **UI is shown big and legible** inside the frame for 3-8 s: a chat bubble, a time stamp ("11:30"), a short reply. The UI *is* the story beats.
5. **Rhythm = reaction.** Cut from UI to a face-sized beat (here: a held focus pull / reveal), back to UI.
6. **Payoff by pulling back.** Last 10 s widen to show the whole world, hold, logo, silence.
7. **No captions fighting the picture.** Words come from the UI itself and from sparse lower-third copy.

## B. "Claude Opus 5.5": the horizon film

What it is: 20 seconds of tactile macro surfaces (tomato skin, blueprint, lace, feather, agate, fabric), every shot framed with a
**curved horizon edge** across the frame. A single serif sentence ("There's / more to / discover") sits *on the horizon line* and
changes words while the surfaces keep cutting. Ends on a quiet dark sky with a sunrise glow and the product name.

Measured (cuts): 0-3 s a held slow dawn horizon; 3.3-15.8 s about 33 cuts, one every 0.3-0.6 s (about 2.7 cuts/s); then a 4 s hold on
"Opus 5.5" -> "Claude" over a sunrise gradient. Each of the three words lasts 3-5 cuts: **text persists across cuts, picture changes
under it.** Serif (Tiempos-like) white, about 8% of frame height, no pill, no box.

Why it works: one visual rule (the horizon), one typographic rule (one serif line on the horizon), maximum variety of
material inside the rule, an acceleration and then a hold, and an end card that returns to the opening image (dawn).

## Transfer to Commission-ai (real footage only)

Commission-ai's own story is "tell it at night, wake up to merged PRs", and the app has a Workshop town with a day/night sky. Both
references have the same arc (dark -> light), so both promos use it.

- **Promo 1, "cinematic story" (from dots), 60 s 16:9.** Night Workshop open from black, title bloom, then the real run told as
  a night shift: composer message -> plan DAG -> agents working -> Needs-you Allow once -> reviewer approval -> PR card -> dawn
  Workshop with a "merged" board. UI shown large, held 3-6 s, slow eased push-ins, warm grade + vignette + bloom + grain, shallow-DoF
  backdrop (blurred copy of the same frame behind a contained panel) so even a 16:9 UI crop feels photographed. Long end pull-back
  from a panel to the full town, then name and tagline.
- **Promo 2, "horizon" (from Opus 5.5), 20-25 s 16:9.** Every shot is real UI or town footage reframed so an *edge* (panel top edge,
  skyline, ground line, card edge) forms the horizon; a serif line sits on it ("Tell it at night. / Wake up to / merged PRs.");
  cut every 0.4-0.6 s through the real UI surfaces on the beat, then hold on a dawn-gradient end card with the name.

Hard rules still apply: no UI edits (grade, blur, vignette, crop are framing, not UI edits), no invented claims, licensed audio only.
